🤔 N-day died, a son was born - N-hour by Anthropic

Salute, I came across a study that Anthropic published and it feels like it corrects the basic assumption of Vulnerability Management

Essentially, the Red Team gave Claude Mythos two artifacts: a public patch diff and two Firefox builds (no source code)

Result

• 18 Firefox patches → 8 working RCE exploits, standalone

• First exploit: less than an hour after the patch was released

• Firefox release with the fix at this moment was still 18 days away

Only stripped binaries and decompiler output. Of 21 kernel bugs: PoC for 18 (fastest in 31 minutes), 8 chains to SYSTEM. The cost of each was ~$2,000

The main mechanism that breaks the old model

Each patch is a map to a vulnerability. Diff between old and new code shows exactly what was broken and where. Previously, it would have taken an expert weeks to turn Diff into a working exploit. That is, with AI models, the very moment of publishing a patch has become the most dangerous: the attacker already knows what to look for, and most systems have not yet been updated

What's changing for Vulnerability Management?

Patching faster is a losing strategy. At 135 CVE per day, the backlog will never disappear. If everything has CVSS 9.8, you haven't prioritized anything. Therefore, you are obliged to assess risks, understand their logic and how reasonable they will be, and also understand architecture - do you understand what I’m getting at, my dear heart?

Numbers that explain

• 2024: average time from patch to exploit - 53 days

• 2026: average time from patch to exploit - less than 24 hours (Zero Day Clock)

• Verizon DBIR 2026: median time to fix known-exploited vulnerabilities - 43 days

• Only 26% of known-exploited CVEs are ever patched

• 135 new CVEs per day, +40% year-on-year

The right questions to ask when assessing information security risks

• Which of these vulnerabilities are actually exploitable in our particular environment?

• Will our current controls stop attempted exploitation?

• Can we prove it?

This is not “patch faster”. This is “to know what exactly is critical for us and check that the protection works.” The very principle of prioritization based on real exploitability, and not on CVSS, is correct regardless of the vendor, and the emphasis on information security risks allows us to move in the right direction accordingly
