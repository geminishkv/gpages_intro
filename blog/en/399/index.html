🤔 Risk- and business-oriented model in information security

GOST R ISO/IEC 15408 and risk management materials always describe the same scheme, which seems to look clear, but still work arises on it, and usually few people explain the principle of its operation.

Starting to work on it, a “risk register” appears in the project without owners, without deadlines and without connection with real assets, which is a common practice for many today.

These elements describe how an organization protects what is valuable, namely assets, from someone who wants to cause damage by exploiting vulnerabilities, and how to counteract this - countermeasures. As a result, half of people call what is actually a vulnerability a threat.

We will describe in detail

• Assets: data, services, business processes, reputation. If you cannot assess the damage from a loss, you do not have an asset, but simply a liability. Example:

- ability to process payments, customer base - assets

- servers, VMs, containers - media, not assets

• Owner: the person or role who is responsible for the asset and makes decisions about its protection, that is, not an IS or administrator. The owner locks in with the asset and makes a decision to accept/minimize/delegate/avoid. Without an owner, the model does not work - there is no one to say “yes, I am ready to live with this risk”

• Threat: a potential event that could damage an asset - a scenario, such as theft of a token, withdrawal of funds through a compromised session. Threat - what could happen; vulnerability is the weakness through which this occurs. A threat without an asset is an abstraction, but without a vulnerability it has not yet been realized

• Attacker: Threat actor - who is capable and motivated to cause damage: insider, APT, etc. Intruder model: category, motivation (finance, intelligence, ideology), resources, access, potential. Without a model of the intruder, countermeasures are selected blindly - you cannot defend against scripts and against groups in the same way

• Attack vectors: the path and method of implementing a threat through vulnerabilities, such as a phishing email, then a macro, load and C2 leak

• Vulnerabilities: a weakness that a threat can exploit, such as a CVE, a process weakness (no triage), an architectural flaw. A vulnerability without a threat is a hypothesis, and without a countermeasure is an open risk.

• Mitigation measures: reduce vulnerabilities, the likelihood of threats occurring or damage to an asset and only work if properly configured and monitored for effectiveness. Principles: defense-in-depth and proportionality. Every minimization measure needs an owner and a metric, otherwise it degrades.

• Risks: the possibility of a threat being realized through a vulnerability with damage to the asset. Vulnerability and threat together are potential, and risk is its assessment in money, time, and consequences. An accepted risk without a deadline and conditions for review is not acceptance, but oblivion to all of us

Typical errors

• Equating threat and vulnerability, as a result the risk register turns into a list of CVEs without scripts

• The server is called an asset, not the data and processes on it

• There is no asset owner and no one to make risk decisions

• Countermeasures are selected by analogy without reference to a specific vulnerability or threat

• Risk assessment without an intruder model, where all threats look equally likely

• We accept the risk without terms and conditions for revision

• Countermeasures are counted based on installation, not functionality

Total

• Assets should be inventoried, owners should be assigned

• Build a model of the offender: category, motivation, resources, access, potential (preferably for cases, namely scenario-based and not according to FSTEC of Russia, otherwise you will get buried)

• Identify threats by catalogs and link them to assets

• Identify vulnerabilities and associate them with assets and threats

• Assess risks using the probability of damage matrix, taking into account the model of the intruder and achievability

• Select countermeasures for vulnerabilities, record those responsible and performance metrics

• Agree on a solution for each risk with the owner

• Maintain accepted risks with review deadlines

• Revise the model when changes occur
