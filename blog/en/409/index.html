🤔 MultiSig for blockchain: real case

Salute,

Today I want to share a case study that was interesting to me and was key for a crypto exchange.

I think such a thing with a non-standard approach will be useful for enriching your experience and seeing practice, so as not to make any additional mistakes, but to see them right away.

Task: it is necessary to ensure the security of 800+ BTC per day passing through the crypto pool. Without a seed phrase on one device, without a single point of failure, without the ability to display everything with one compromised signature.

How the transaction works

The BTC on the address is always spent entirely - it is divided into several outgoing addresses, the remainder goes to a new address (change output), the difference between the incoming and outgoing amounts is a commission. The address contains a hash in the chain and allows you to allow specific coins to be spent. Multisignature changes this hash: instead of "one signature" it becomes "M of N signatures"

M-of-N schemes

• 2/3 - hot wallet of the exchange. The exchange holds one key, the second is a reserve key, and the third is held by the cybersecurity team. A minimum of two is required to confirm a transaction. The security team signs only after checking for AML and kill-switch

• 3/5 – wallet with a low level of trust. Any three of the five owners can spend. Anyone can initiate a transfer, but execute it only when the required number of signatures is reached. Reduces three risks at the same time: embezzlement by one participant, hacking, loss of access

• 2/3 – escrow scheme. Buyer, seller, arbitrator. Normally, the deal is closed with the signatures of both parties without an arbitrator. In case of a dispute, the arbitrator signs in favor of one of the parties. The platform physically cannot withdraw money unilaterally

Case solution - how is it implemented in practice?

• Signature scheme: 2/3 with separation of roles - the automatic service signs routine payments, the security key is isolated and connected only for non-standard operations or large withdrawals above the AML threshold

• Checks before signing: validating the recipient’s address against the whitelist, checking the amount, checking that the change output returns to our controlled address and does not go somewhere else

• Key storage: hot key in HSM on the server, backup key offline in physically isolated storage, security key from the information security team with hardware token PKCS#11 (at that time). No seed phrase in plaintext, no keys in ENV variables

• Monitoring: each signed transaction is logged with metadata - who signed, at what time, amount, recipient address. Alert when signing above threshold and other AML checks

Why is this more important than it seems?

• Most hacks are not a blockchain hack, but a compromise of a single key: ENV leak, server hack, insider, etc. Therefore, Multisig takes the question to the level of compromise M out of N at the same time - this is a fundamentally different level of attack

• For fintech products, this is also an architectural argument: if the platform is physically unable to sign a transaction unilaterally, it is not only safer, but also legally cleaner in terms of liability

Total (read the full case with transaction analysis and diagrams here)

• Multisig is not a feature, but an architectural principle: M out of N owners must agree on the operation

• 2/3 – the minimum reasonable threshold for a hot wallet with real money

• 5/7 - recommended threshold for cold storage and distribution of funds from nodes

• Separation of roles, such as automatic service/security/backup reduces both operational and information security risks at the same time

• Whitelist of addresses with threshold checks connected to monitoring are independent layers on top of the multi-signature itself

• Compromising one key with a 2/3 scheme, and especially Escrow, is problematic

Footnote

• Address hash (locking script) - each unspent transaction output is protected by a condition

• M-of-N (threshold signature) - scheme where N is the total number of participants with keys, M is the minimum number of signatures to complete the operation

• Escrow - escrow mechanism: funds are blocked with a neutral third party until the terms of the transaction are fulfilled
